Mandibular Fracture Reduction Planning

Graduate research that turned dental occlusion from an implicit surgical judgment into a computable constraint for virtual jawbone reduction.

Verified research 2021.03 – 2023.02 · M.S. thesis

At a glance

Fracture fragments could be moved into many geometrically plausible positions. I used the upper dental arch and occlusion relationship to define a reproducible planning target.

Decision Timeline

Uncertainty

What defines a correct virtual reduction?

Bone surfaces alone did not uniquely encode how the teeth should meet after reduction.

Probe

Represent the arch line and occlusal relationship

I tested whether a dental-arch model could provide a stable target for moving fracture fragments.

Evidence

Occlusion constrained otherwise ambiguous motion

Prepared cases showed that the dental relationship could guide reduction toward a reproducible final configuration.

Decision

Optimize around dental occlusion

I formulated the reduction objective around the upper arch and contact relationship rather than surface matching alone.

Integration

Embed the method in a planning workflow

The algorithm and 3D interaction were implemented in a medical-image planning environment for repeatable experiments.

Verified Outcome

Method survived peer review

The work became an M.S. thesis, an international conference paper, and a peer-reviewed journal article.
