At a glance

The hard part was not rendering a hologram. It was preserving a trustworthy spatial chain across camera, medical-image, patient, instrument, and HoloLens coordinates while learning a specialized SDK quickly enough to meet the program goal.

My responsibility
Coordinate mathematics, SDK probes, filtering decision, integration, and demonstration
Evidence
Demonstration and review passed; quantitative program objective achieved
Constraint
Specialized SDK and domain integration with no suitable internal precedent

Decision Timeline

Uncertainty

Could every coordinate frame be made consistent?

Camera, medical-image, rigid-body, patient, instrument, and headset frames each had different conventions. A visually plausible overlay could still be mathematically wrong.

Probe

Break the SDK into parallel micro-PoCs

I created four to five small services for eye tracking, screen-space validation, data transfer, and other critical SDK behavior. Most feature feasibility checks took one to two days.

Evidence

Raw gaze points were unstable

Visualized samples showed that normal eye motion produced large jumps. I treated far-out observations as noise relative to the recent ten-frame mean.

Decision

Filter before interaction and derive transforms explicitly

I excluded samples too far from the current window mean and implemented the coordinate chain from rigid-transform equations rather than relying on implicit engine behavior.

Integration

Merge only the proven SDK functions

Validated probes were integrated into the main surgical-navigation software. Final integration typically took one to two weeks after selection.

Verified Outcome

Accepted demonstration, achieved objective

The system passed demonstration and review, met the quantitative project goal, appeared in the final report, and received favorable evaluation during the formal demonstration.
