At a glance

The main engineering work was not just building scenes. It was understanding how surgeons judge midline, overjet, and overbite; how an articulator is used; and when and how instruments enter—then making those decisions visible in software.

Decision Timeline

Uncertainty

Could clinical intent survive translation into XR?

Terms such as midline, overjet, overbite, instrument entry timing, and articulator workflow had to become precise software behavior.

Probe

Show the workflow as a working 3D prototype

I wrote scenarios and built rapid prototypes for navigation and consultation instead of validating requirements only through documents.

Evidence

Clinical experts corrected the visible sequence

Working views made it possible to review 3D model placement, slide order, anatomical emphasis, and the timing and direction of tools.

Decision

Use clinical criteria as interaction requirements

I encoded occlusal measures and procedural checkpoints into the scenario rather than presenting generic anatomy.

Integration

Unify navigation and consultation narratives

Approved scenarios were integrated into XR navigation and an orthognathic-surgery consultation flow with clinical 3D models.

Verified Outcome

Approved, registered, adopted, and research-tested

Requirements were approved, the software was registered, the demonstration led to adoption, and the system progressed to patient-consented testing and surveys.
