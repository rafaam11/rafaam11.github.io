C-arm Navigation Feasibility

A focused feasibility study for relating tracked instruments and C-arm imaging geometry without overstating the maturity of the result.

At a glance

The project asked whether a tracked instrument could be expressed relative to the image geometry strongly enough to support a navigation concept.

My responsibility
Coordinate analysis, feasibility setup, visualization
Evidence
Technical feasibility demonstrated in a controlled setup
State
Early research exploration

Decision Timeline

Uncertainty

Could live tracking be related to projection geometry?

The imaging frame and tracker frame had no useful relationship until calibration and reference definitions were made explicit.

Probe

Build a minimal tracked-instrument scene

I separated device geometry, tracking input, and image projection to test each relationship independently.

Evidence

Controlled poses exposed the transform behavior

Known instrument motions produced consistent visual changes after the coordinate chain was defined.

Decision

Limit the claim to feasibility

The evidence supported the coordinate concept, but not a clinical navigation workflow or accuracy claim.

Integration

Package the coordinate path as a demonstrator

The calibrated relationships and overlay were combined into a repeatable internal demonstration.

Verified Outcome

Coordinate concept demonstrated

The prototype showed that tracked instrument pose could be visualized relative to C-arm geometry in the controlled setup.

My Decisions

  • Defined the frames and calibration path.
  • Designed the controlled-pose test.
  • Kept the evidence claim at feasibility level.

Team Result

  • The team gained a concrete basis for deciding whether deeper navigation R&D was justified.
  • Open accuracy questions were made explicit.

Current Status

Research feasibility only. No clinical validation, accuracy certification, or production deployment is claimed.
