At a glance

The technical question was whether live coil pose, patient registration, and medical-image targets could be made legible enough for research navigation without hiding coordinate assumptions inside the UI.

My responsibility
Coordinate model, tracked-object integration, navigation visualization
Evidence
Working research prototype and repeatable navigation flow
State
Research—not a clinical outcome claim

Decision Timeline

Uncertainty

Can the planned target and live coil share a reliable frame?

Tracker measurements, image coordinates, and patient anatomy needed an explicit relationship before guidance could be meaningful.

Probe

Isolate tracking and visualization

I first connected tracked rigid bodies to a simple 3D scene, then tested image-space registration and target visualization separately.

Evidence

The transform chain was observable

Rendering each intermediate frame made axis and reference mistakes visible before they reached the full navigation view.

Decision

Keep registration explicit

I represented tracker-to-patient and patient-to-image transforms as distinct steps and exposed their result in the interface.

Integration

Combine medical planning and live pose

The validated tracking path was integrated with 3D Slicer/VTK visualization for target-relative coil guidance.

Verified Outcome

Research navigation flow demonstrated

The prototype supported repeatable visualization of the planned target and tracked coil within the registered patient model.

My Decisions

  • Separated the coordinate transforms.
  • Chose intermediate-frame visualization as the debugging method.
  • Integrated tracking with the medical-image interface.

Team Result

  • The research team gained a concrete navigation prototype.
  • Coordinate assumptions became inspectable rather than implicit.

Current Status

Completed as a research prototype. The evidence supports technical feasibility and a repeatable flow, not clinical efficacy or commercial deployment.
