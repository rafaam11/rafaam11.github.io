Decision Timeline

Uncertainty

How could safety logic be tested without constant vehicle access?

Sensor blind spots, coordinate mistakes, and acceleration/deceleration gates often surfaced only after a long trip to the vehicle.

Probe

Build a LiteSim for each critical part

I first created small validators for SafetyGate behavior, vision recognition, and LiDAR/ToF obstacle sensing rather than one large simulator.

Evidence

Visual overlays made hidden errors discussable

The tools exposed sensor coverage, blind zones, obstacle regions, coordinate frames, and speed-gate transitions before field deployment.

Decision

Use simulation as a pre-field decision gate

I introduced LiteSim to the team for early checks and management demonstrations, while retaining the real vehicle as the final evidence source.

Integration

Move from personal tool to team workflow

As the validators proved useful, the development team adopted them for communication, reporting, and expected-issue checks.

Verified Outcome

Fewer trips, faster shared decisions

Typical Daegu-to-Chungcheong field travel moved from one or two trips per week to roughly one or two per month, while the team could review safety and sensing behavior earlier.

Integration

Deploy and operate on real production sites

From 2025 I worked on-site with an OEM partner's vehicles — teaching, operating, and scoping our own solution. A second field contract then shipped with our fork-control, obstacle-detection, and vision modules running in production.

Current State

Full in-house RCS on a Zenoh network

The current deployment replaces everything above the lowest-level vehicle control with our in-house stack. I bridged the NAV350 and safety LiDAR onto the Zenoh network, own the safety-policy manager, and am validating a truck-loading vision module (ToF+RGB with SAM-family segmentation) driven by behavior-tree actions.

Current Status

The platform is ongoing. Reduced travel, team adoption, and a delivered second field contract are observed; the full in-house RCS deployment and the loading-vision module are in progress, and claims about their long-term stability or maintainability remain expected outcomes, not yet longitudinally measured.
