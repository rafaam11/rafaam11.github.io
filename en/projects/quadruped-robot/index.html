Mechanically Actuated Quadruped Robot

An undergraduate mechatronics project that connected linkage design, embedded control, fabrication, and motion tests in one working prototype.

Completed 2020.04 – 2021.01 · Undergraduate project

Linkage DesignEmbedded ControlCADFabrication

At a glance

The project tested whether a multi-joint linkage could turn limited actuator motion into a practical quadruped gait while remaining fabricable by a student team.

My responsibility
Mechanism concept, integration, motion testing, technical documentation
Evidence
Working prototype, engineering-fair award, patent application
State
Completed educational prototype

Decision Timeline

Uncertainty

Could the desired gait be achieved with a simple mechanism?

Actuator count, linkage geometry, weight, and fabrication limits competed with one another.

Probe

Prototype the leg path

We tested linkage proportions and actuator sequences before committing to the full body.

Evidence

Physical motion exposed CAD-only assumptions

Early assemblies revealed clearance, balance, and timing issues that were not obvious from the model alone.

Decision

Favor reliable linkage behavior

I prioritized a fabricable joint structure and repeatable gait over mechanical complexity.

Integration

Combine mechanism and control

The linkage, embedded sequence, chassis, and power constraints were tuned as one system.

Verified Outcome

Working prototype and formal recognition

The robot operated as a complete prototype, received an engineering-fair award, and supported a patent application.

My Decisions

Team Result

Current Status

Completed undergraduate prototype. It demonstrates mechatronic integration and learning velocity, not modern autonomous locomotion performance.
